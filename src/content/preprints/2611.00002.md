# Topic Modeling of the Generative AI and Large Language Model Ethics and Governance Literature Using Latent Dirichlet Allocation, HJ-Biplot, and LDAvis

## 1. Introduction

Scholarship on the ethical, social, and regulatory implications of artificial intelligence (AI) has grown from principle-setting exercises into critical assessments of practical implementation and analyses of the specific risks posed by large language models (LLMs). The public release of accessible generative AI systems has accelerated this output: **82.8% of the documents in this corpus were published in 2025 and 2026**[cite: 1].

Previous mappings relied largely on qualitative reviews of policy guidelines or narrow domain studies. To address the scale of tens of thousands of scholarly records, this study builds a reproducible corpus, selects an optimal Latent Dirichlet Allocation (LDA) model via multiple coherence and structural diagnostics, quantifies solution robustness across seeds, neighbouring $K$, and Dirichlet priors, and maps the thematic space using HJ-Biplots and LDAvis[cite: 1].

## 2. Materials & Methods

Records were retrieved from **Scopus** (11 October 2026) intersecting generative AI/LLM terms with governance, ethics, regulation, and policy terms for English publications (2018–2026), capped at 20,000 records[cite: 1].

### Screening Summary

- **Records identified (Scopus):** 20,000[cite: 1]
- **Excluded by length (< 20 words):** 495[cite: 1]
- **DOI / Title duplicates (flagged, retained):** 20 / 59[cite: 1]
- **Final modeling corpus:** 19,505 documents[cite: 1]
- **Countries / Distinct sources:** 86 / 5,697[cite: 1]

### Text Preprocessing Pipeline

1. **Normalization & Cleaning:** Unicode normalization, lowercasing, and removal of URLs, emails, numeric citations, and editorial/license notices[cite: 1].
2. **Lemmatization:** Whitespace tokenization followed by WordNet lemmatization (nouns, then verbs) without stemming to preserve interpretability[cite: 1].
3. **Four-Layer Stopword Removal (288 terms):** English functional stopwords, editorial terms, methodological terms, and generic domain stopwords, while retaining key acronyms (`ai`, `llm`, `gpt`, `nlp`, `gdpr`)[cite: 1].
4. **Bigram Detection:** Gensim phrase scoring (min count 59, threshold 8) producing phrases such as `generative_ai` and `machine_learning`[cite: 1].
5. **Vocabulary Filtering:** Pruning terms appearing in $< 39$ documents or $> 50\%$ of documents, yielding **4,533 training terms** across **2,361,836 total tokens** (mean 121.09 tokens/document)[cite: 1].

## 3. Model Selection & Results

Evaluating $K \in [4, 40]$ (symmetric $\alpha$, 30 passes), $K = 27$ was selected as the smallest $K$ within 1% of the maximum $C_v$ coherence ($K = 40$, $C_v = 0.5696$) with high diversity and low dominance ($< 40\%$)[cite: 1]. Optimizing over a 16-combination $\alpha/\eta$ prior grid yielded the final configuration: **asymmetric $\alpha$**, **$\eta = 0.5$**, **50 passes**, and **seed 42**[cite: 1].

### Thematic Blocks & Two-Layer Structure

The 27 topics divide into a large **Technical Layer** (sharing dense overlapping vocabulary) and a smaller, distinct **Normative Layer**, grouped into five thematic blocks by dominant-topic counts[cite: 1]:

1. **Applications and Evaluation (30.1% | 5,873 docs):** Clinical/healthcare AI (T9), education and student learning (T27), training/fine-tuning and unlearning (T14), evaluation benchmarks (T13), intelligent energy/market systems (T15), and domain reviews (T23)[cite: 1].
2. **Technical Core (22.7% | 4,429 docs):** Human problem-solving (T1), ChatGPT response evaluation (T5), text detection and classification (T19), biological sequence foundation models (T10), ChatGPT in literature reviews (T25), and multimodal/vision-language models (T12)[cite: 1].
3. **Governance and Ethics (20.1% | 3,918 docs):** Generative AI ethics and societal challenges (T3), AI governance frameworks (T2), bias/fairness/stereotypes in prompts (T8), adoption perception surveys (T6), and multilingual/cultural corpora (T24)[cite: 1].
4. **Interaction and Agents (18.0% | 3,505 docs):** Retrieval, knowledge graphs, and RAG (T20), conversational chatbots and mental health support (T17), agentic/multi-agent enterprise workflows (T7), human cognition/emotion simulation (T18), and robotics/digital twins (T21)[cite: 1].
5. **Risk, Security, and Policy (9.1% | 1,780 docs):** Law, regulation, and compliance (T26), safety, cybersecurity, and adversarial robustness (T11), value alignment and moral decision-making (T22), privacy and explainability (T4), and public policy/urban platforms (T16)[cite: 1].

## 4. Key Findings & Diagnostics

- **Bibliometric & Geographic Concentration:** Articles (42.65%) and conference papers (41.07%) dominate[cite: 1]. The **United States (23.71%)** and **China (20.32%)** contribute **44.0%** of all documents[cite: 1]; China leads technical/model-building topics (T10, T12, T14, T15, T20, T21, T23, T24), while Western countries cluster around ethics, governance, and legal compliance (T2, T3, T26)[cite: 1].
- **HJ-Biplot & LDAvis Convergence:** The `years × topics` biplot (95.6% variance retained; mean $\cos^2 = 0.900$) captures a temporal trajectory from early human problem-solving, text detection, and bias (2022–2023) toward retrieval/RAG, agentic systems, evaluation, and governance frameworks (2025–2026)[cite: 1]. LDAvis ($\lambda = 0.6$) confirms tight lexical clustering among technical topics and clearer separation for normative topics[cite: 1].
- **Sensitivity & Literature Gaps:** Across five seeds ($42\text{–}46$), stability is moderate (**mean Jaccard = 0.319**, **mean ARI = 0.241**), and neighbouring $K \in \{25, 26, 28, 29\}$ recover 8–11 of the 27 reference topics at Jaccard $\ge 0.5$[cite: 1]. High-volume topics are robust, whereas low-volume topics (e.g., T16) should be treated as provisional[cite: 1]. Notably, no dedicated topic emerged for environmental footprint or labor-market impacts[cite: 1].
